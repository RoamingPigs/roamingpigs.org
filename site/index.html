Independent AI systems research

Research notes with the limits printed next to the numbers.

I run small experiments on speech models, verifier ensembles and local inference hardware. This is the public record: what I ran, what came out, how big the sample was, and what the result does not show.

How to read a number here

Measured
I ran it and recorded it: a model on audio, a meter on a wall socket. It describes that run and nothing wider.
Modelled
A formula or a calibrated estimate, not a reading. Good for planning, wrong in ways I try to name.
Simulated
Data a script generated from settings I chose. It shows how a method behaves, not how the world does.
Proposed
A question and a plan. No result yet.

Speech models break down on radio-quality audio

Measured

Six open speech models transcribed five scripted maritime radio calls through six audio paths and five noise levels. Averaged over every path, word error was 59% with no added noise and 129% at 0 dB. Above 100% means the models wrote more wrong words than the call contained.

What this does not show

Five utterances is a pilot. The speech is synthetic (text to speech), not recorded radio traffic. There are no confidence intervals, so small gaps between codecs mean nothing yet. Decoding was greedy with a 48-token cap, and error above 100% includes inserted words. It says nothing about any product.

Run
2026-10-10 18:34 UTC
Sample
6 models (39M to 809M parameters) × 5 utterances × 6 audio paths × 5 noise levels = 900 transcripts
Method
Word error rate against the script, greedy decoding, no resampling

Given silence, every model still wrote words

Measured

On 24 clips of silence, static or radio squelch, each of 6 models wrote words on 24 of 24. A basic energy gate removed the words on both silence conditions. It did nothing on any of the six noise and squelch conditions, so 18 of 24 clips still produced text.

What this does not show

The clips are 2.5 seconds of generated audio, three seeds per condition, one gate setting (−42 dBFS). Real radio recordings may behave differently. The same models can be quiet with a better gate or a different decoder. Peak speed reached 160 words per minute in one noise condition, which is a single cell, not a typical rate.

Run
2026-10-10 17:48 UTC
Sample
6 models × 8 conditions × 3 seeds, 2.5 s each
Method
Count clips where the transcript contains any word. Gate: 20 ms frames, −42 dBFS threshold

e1-hallucination-ledger.json

What a local model costs in energy, read off a wall meter

MeasuredModelled

On one 122.7 GB unified-memory machine, a wall meter read 2.54 to 3.81 joules per generated token across two models. Before I ran the meter, a calibrated model of mine had predicted 2.82 for the smallest. The meter read 3.81.

What this does not show

Each number is one run of 6 to 10 seconds and 384 tokens. Energy per token uses mean wall power. Peak power gives higher values, up to 5.23 for the first run. This is one machine at batch size one. Serving many requests at once can lower energy per token several-fold, so this is not a comparison with data centre hardware. A third run, on a 125.1B mixture model, is left out. Its recorded speed implies 356 GB/s of memory traffic on a 256 GB/s bus, so its speed or its active-parameter count is wrong in the ledger. The calculator shows the assumptions.

Run
2026-10-10 23:26 UTC
Sample
Two runs, one per model, 384 generated tokens each
Method
Smart-plug wall power averaged over the run, divided by tokens per second. Weights at Q4_K_M

sovereign-apu-inference-ledger.json

Why voting across verifiers can mislead

Simulated

In a simulation, two code verifiers made mistakes on the same plausible-looking bugs, so their errors correlated at 0.75 (covariance 0.093). When errors overlap like that, counting agreement overstates confidence. This illustrates the concern behind the method. It does not measure real verifiers.

What this does not show

A seeded script generated the candidates and the verifier scores from distributions I chose. The correlation is a property of those settings. A study with real model-written candidates and real verifiers is a separate piece of work. This record covers the simulation only.

Do coordinated AI coding agents beat one agent?

Measured

On 8 small Python tasks, with 16 attempts per arm, a single agent produced 8 accepted solutions. Ordinary two-worker messaging produced 0. Echo, the coordination layer I build, also produced 0, with Git merge conflicts in 9 of 16 attempts. No coordination benefit is established.

What this does not show

The tasks were reused and the agents may have seen them, so this is a diagnostic, not a benchmark. Transport timings measure messaging speed, not task success. A coordination benefit would need fresh tasks and repeated runs.

Run
2026-10-10 17:40 UTC
Sample
8 tasks × 3 arms, 16 attempts per arm (48 in all)
Method
An attempt is accepted when all hidden acceptance checks pass
